## What official Indian government bodies/institutes document organic/traditional pest-and-disease management?

### Takeaway
Three distinct official bodies matter: (1) the **National Centre for Organic and Natural Farming (NCONF)**, formerly the National Centre of Organic Farming (NCOF), under the Ministry of Agriculture, plus its network of **Regional Centres of Organic Farming (RCOFs)**; (2) ICAR's **All India Network Programme on Organic Farming (AI-NPOF)** and the related **AICRP on Integrated Farming Systems**, both coordinated by **ICAR-IIFSR (Indian Institute of Farming Systems Research), Modipuram**; and (3) **NIPHM (National Institute of Plant Health Management), Hyderabad**, which holds statutory Central Insecticide Laboratory functions for biopesticide quality testing. These are genuine primary/institutional sources, distinct from commercial or blog content.

### Cited Findings
- The National Centre of Organic Farming was renamed the **National Centre for Organic and Natural Farming (NCONF), Ghaziabad**, in March 2022, with five regional centres — [journalsofindia.com (sourced from PIB & NCOF)](https://journalsofindia.com/?p=7585); site itself at `pgsindia-ncof.gov.in` — [About Us page](https://pgsindia-ncof.gov.in/about-us) (page exists per search index; could not be fetched directly in this session due to tool network failure).
- NCOF/NCONF's mandate includes encouraging production of certified organic inputs — "bio-fertilizers, organic manures, compost etc. and bio-pesticides, bio-control agents etc." — and it maintains the **National and Regional culture collection bank of biofertilizer, biocontrol, and waste-decomposer organisms** that supplies production units, and develops **standards and testing protocols for organic agricultural inputs** — [journalsofindia.com summary of NCOF](https://journalsofindia.com/national-centre-for-organic-farming-ncof/?print=pdf); cross-referenced at [india.gov.in organic farming topic page](https://www.india.gov.in/topics/agriculture/organic-farming).
- **Regional Centres of Organic Farming (RCOFs)**, including one in Bangalore, were set up under the 10th Five Year Plan as a pilot and act as nodal agencies for "formulating standards and accreditation mechanisms, and for inspection, regulation, quality control and monitoring." NCOF and the six RCOFs are notified as **authorized quality-analysis laboratories for organic fertilizers and bio-fertilizers under the Fertilizer Control Order (FCO)**, each with testing capacity cited at ~400 samples/year — [eparlib.nic.in parliamentary document](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/546903/1/46553.pdf). Note: the search did not turn up confirmation that RCOFs specifically test *biopesticides* (as opposed to biofertilizers/organic manure) under the Insecticide Act — that remains unconfirmed, see Gaps.
- **ICAR-IIFSR (Indian Institute of Farming Systems Research, Modipuram, Meerut)** coordinates two relevant ICAR schemes via its Project Coordination Unit: the **AICRP on Integrated Farming Systems** (74 centres per the 2019-20 annual report: 25 main on-station, 11 sub on-station, 6 voluntary on-station, 32 on-farm) and the **All India Network Programme on Organic Farming (AI-NPOF)**, which began as the "Network Project on Organic Farming" in 2004-05 and runs 20 cooperating centres across 16 states — [IIFSR PCU page](https://iifsr.icar.gov.in/PCU.html); [NPOF Annual Report 2019-20 PDF](https://iifsr.icar.gov.in/icar-iifsr/npof/data/uploads/files/NPOF_Annual%20report_2019-20.pdf); [NPOF Annual Report 2017-18 PDF](https://iifsr.icar.gov.in/icar-iifsr/npof/data/uploads/files/Annual_Report_NPOF_2017-18.pdf).
- AI-NPOF's stated research aims include "developing efficient crop and soil management options for organic farming" and studying productivity/quality/input-use-efficiency of crops under organic systems — [journalsofindia.com summary](https://journalsofindia.com/all-india-network-programme-on-organic-farming-ai-npof/?print=pdf) (sourced from ICAR).
- A 2022-23 university-hosted list of ICAR network-project coordinators names **Dr. Dhananjay K. Singh** as PC/PI for the Organic Farming network project under Agronomy — [G.B. Pant University project list PDF](https://www.gbpuat.ac.in/directorates/directorate_experiment/10.12.2022_Project_ongoing_2022-23.pdf) (2022-23 snapshot; may be outdated by 2026).
- **NIPHM (National Institute of Plant Health Management), Hyderabad** has a Gazette-notified quality control laboratory and "has been given the functions of a Central Insecticide Laboratory for biopesticide testing under section 16 of the Insecticide Act, 1968." It runs dedicated training courses on "quality control of microbial biopesticides" (one instance scheduled 15–19 September 2025) — [niphm.gov.in training brochure](https://niphm.gov.in/Training/qcmb26.pdf); [niphm.gov.in education brochure](https://niphm.gov.in/Education/QCMBBRO25.pdf).
- A separate, newer facility: Union Agriculture Minister inaugurated an **Integrated Biological Control Laboratory in Hyderabad** (reported via Krishi Jagran and other ag-news outlets, not yet independently confirmed on an .gov.in primary page in this session) — [Krishi Jagran](https://krishijagran.com/news/tomar-inaugurates-integrated-biological-control-lab-in-hyderabad/?amp=1); [Indian Chemical News](https://www.indianchemicalnews.com/research/agriculture-minister-tomar-inaugurates-integrated-biological-control-laboratory-in-hyderabad-17475); [Global Agriculture](https://www.global-agriculture.com/india-region/union-agriculture-and-farmers-welfare-minister-inaugurates-integrated-biological-control-laboratory-in-hyderabad/). This is directly relevant to an Andhra Pradesh/Telangana-region focus since it is sited in Hyderabad, but treat the specific claims as secondary-reported until verified on a .gov.in page.

### Inferences
- For an Andhra Pradesh-specific organic-treatment content pipeline, the most load-bearing *official* bodies to cite/link are NCONF (national standards/culture collections), ICAR-IIFSR's AI-NPOF (the only dedicated organic-farming agronomic research network), and NIPHM Hyderabad (statutory biopesticide quality authority, regionally located near AP/Telangana) — not generic organic-farming advocacy sites.
- The RCOF network is primarily a *certification/standards* body (FCO testing of fertilizers/manures) rather than a repository of disease-treatment protocols per se; it should not be cited as a source of specific disease-treatment doses without further verification.

### Gaps

- No AP-specific state body (e.g., an Andhra Pradesh organic-farming cell or ANGRAU-specific organic unit) was confirmed distinct from the national ICAR/NCONF structure in this search pass.

## Does TKDL cover agricultural/crop-protection traditional knowledge, or is it focused on medicinal Ayurveda?

### Takeaway
**TKDL's core, longstanding, and publicly documented scope is medicinal — Ayurveda, Unani, Siddha and Sowa Rigpa formulations for human health — not crop protection or agriculture.** There is a very recent (reported December 2025) move by CSIR to *begin* extending TKDL to include traditional agricultural practices, but this is described as a new/emerging effort, not yet an established public-facing agricultural database. **TKDL should not be presented in the app as a current source of crop-disease treatment knowledge.**

### Cited Findings
- TKDL's established description: "a collection of the medicinal formulations available in the ancient texts of Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani and Yoga," set up in 2001 by the Ministry of AYUSH with CSIR as implementing agency — [Wikipedia: Traditional Knowledge Digital Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Knowledge_Digital_Library) (secondary, but consistent with all other sources found).
- A Virtual Health Library (BVS) portal listing describes TKDL as "a representative database of Ayurvedic, Unani, Siddha and Sowarigpa formulations... selected from various classical texts," using "plant, animal or mineral ingredients to address various disease conditions" — i.e., explicitly human-disease-oriented — [BVSalud portal listing](https://bvsalud.org/portal-lis/en/lis-search/resource/?id=49735).
- TKDL's stated purpose is defensive patent protection — "to prevent erroneous patent grants on Indian traditional knowledge" — and it is offered **only by subscription to patent offices**, not as an open public crop-treatment database — per the same BVSalud/secondary summaries and [WIPO TKDL presentation](https://www.wipo.org/edocs/mdocs/pct/en/pct_mia_25/pct_mia_25_presentation_indian_tkdl.pdf).
- A December-2025-dated article/paper is titled **"Digitization of Indian Traditional Agricultural Practices in the Traditional Knowledge Digital Library"** (Annals of Library and Information Studies, vol. 72), indicating CSIR/TKDL is pursuing an expansion into agricultural traditional knowledge — [or.niscpr.res.in abstract/listing](https://or.niscpr.res.in/index.php/ALIS/article/view/25672); [download link](https://or.niscpr.res.in/index.php/ALIS/article/download/25672/5137/109609). This is an academic paper describing/proposing the expansion, not evidence that a public, crop-protection-specific TKDL module is already live and queryable.
- The one concrete historical link between TKDL/traditional-knowledge defense and crop protection is the well-known **neem patent case** — USDA/W.R. Grace's EPO patent on a method of controlling fungi using neem oil extracts, which was challenged (though this predates and is adjacent to, rather than part of, TKDL itself) — cited via a UPSC-notes secondary source, [legacyias.com](https://www.legacyias.com/traditional-knowledge-upsc-notes/) (treat as secondary/non-authoritative for legal specifics).

### Inferences
- TKDL is **not currently a usable source for an "organic treatment" app feature on crop diseases**. Its public value is as a patent-defense tool for medicinal (human-health) formulations, and any agriculture-related expansion is, as of late 2025, at the research/proposal stage, not a queryable public resource.
- The neem-fungicide patent history is relevant context (it illustrates India's documented traditional neem-based disease-control knowledge existed and was contested), but it is a historical/legal episode, not a source of citable treatment protocols or doses.

### Gaps
- Could not directly access `tkdl.res.in` to confirm its current public-facing scope statement verbatim (WebFetch network failure); all findings here are from secondary descriptions and one academic paper, not the live TKDL homepage.
- Could not determine whether the December 2025 CSIR agricultural-TKDL initiative has produced any public output yet, or what its scope/timeline is.

## Are there published ICAR/agricultural-university bulletins specifically on biopesticides/biocontrol agents with application rates, that are peer-reviewed or institutionally published?

### Takeaway
Yes — ICAR's own journal repository (**epubs.icar.org.in**) hosts peer-reviewed primary research with specific doses for Trichoderma spp. and Pseudomonas fluorescens against named crop diseases, and a parallel **government regulatory document** (DPPQS "Major Uses of Pesticides... Bio-Insecticides," dated 31.03.2024) gives official application-rate tables for registered biopesticide products. These are genuinely primary/institutional, in contrast to commercial product-listing sites (e.g., napanta.com) which repeat label-style doses without citable institutional authorship.

### Cited Findings
- **ICAR-Tripura study** (published via epubs.icar.org.in, Indian Phytopathological Journal): "Evaluation of biological control agents against sheath blight of rice in Tripura" — found *Pseudomonas fluorescens* effective when applied to soil with FYM and as a foliar spray at a **5% solution of 2 × 10⁹ cfu** — [epubs.icar.org.in / IPPJ article](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/28116).
- **ICAR-IARI / Indian Journal of Nematology (2024)**: seed treatment of mungbean with *Trichoderma harzianum* and *P. fluorescens* at **1% and 2% w/w**, alone or combined with carbofuran, tested against reniform nematode; the combination of **Th and Ps at 1% each with carbofuran at 1 kg a.i./ha** significantly reduced nematode multiplication — [epubs.icar.org.in / IJN article](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJN/article/view/160220).
- A Rajasthan field study (multi-year) on groundnut found that **seed treatment with *Trichoderma viride* plus two foliar sprays of 5% neem seed kernel extract (NSKE) at 45 and 60 days after sowing** gave the best control of collar rot, and reduced early leaf spot by **60.54%** relative to control — [epubs.icar.org.in / IJAgS article](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/download/87012/35570/222786) (note: this appears to be an ICAR journal-hosted study from Rajasthan, not an ANGRAU/Andhra Pradesh trial — flagged so as not to misattribute it to AP).
- A second, separate groundnut study on ICAR's repository, "Development of biological control strategies for collar rot and early leaf spot for organic cultivation of groundnut," is indexed but its specific dose content was not retrieved in this session — [epubs.icar.org.in / IPPJ article](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IPPJ/article/view/58373) (title/existence confirmed; content not yet extracted — flagged as a gap).
- **Official regulatory dose table**: The Directorate of Plant Protection, Quarantine & Storage (DPPQS, under CIBRC) publishes a periodically updated **"Major Uses of Pesticides/Insecticides/Bio-Insecticides"** document (edition "as on 31.03.2024," and a newer edition "as on 30.09.2025" was also found mirrored by a university). Per the search index, the 31.03.2024 edition "lists bio-insecticide uses by crop, pest, dose per hectare, dilution, and waiting period" and explicitly states it is "compiled on the basis of available information for guidance and not for legal purposes" — [ppqs.gov.in PDF (31.03.2024)](https://ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/major_uses_of_pesticides_insecticides_bio_insecticides_as_on_31.03.2024.pdf); newer mirrored edition (30.09.2025 data) — [new.jau.in mirror](https://new.jau.in/storage/document/dynamic_document//residue_bio_insecticide_03102025_20251226_779.pdf). **Could not fetch either PDF's full content in this session (WebFetch DNS failures on both the .gov.in original and the university mirror)** — only the search-index description of its structure was obtained, so specific per-crop dose numbers from this specific document are not yet confirmed and should be pulled by a session with working fetch access before citing exact figures from it.
- A separate official **bio-fungicide label-style document** (`ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/bio-fungicide_31.3.2024.pdf`) was indexed showing: ***Pseudomonas fluorescens* 1.75% WP** as seed treatment at roughly **5 g per kg of seed**; foliar use at about **2.5 kg/ha in 500 L water**, and **3 kg/ha for tomato early blight** — [ppqs.gov.in bio-fungicide PDF](https://ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/bio-fungicide_31.3.2024.pdf). Flagged explicitly: **this document could not be directly fetched/verified in this session** (DNS failure); the dose figures above come only from a WebSearch tool's auto-generated summary of the PDF, not from this researcher's own reading of the primary text. Treat as provisional pending direct verification.
- Commercial product-listing site **napanta.com** (a pesticide-info/e-commerce site, NOT an institutional source) lists *Trichoderma viride* 1% WP doses: seed treatment ~4-5 g/kg seed; soil treatment 2.5 kg (1% WP) mixed with 62.5 kg FYM broadcast per hectare; foliar for sheath blight 2.5 kg/ha in 500 L water, 3 sprays at 15-day intervals from 30 days after planting — [napanta.com product pages](https://www.napanta.com/pesticide-details/pid3014) (and similar pid30XX pages). **Explicitly flagged as a secondary/commercial source**, included only to show what generic dose claims circulate; do not attribute these doses to ICAR or any institute.
- A peer-reviewed chickpea study (2022, PMC) tested *Trichoderma viride* powder and tablet formulations against Fusarium wilt at recommended, double, and half-recommended dosages (exact recommended-dose figure not retrieved) — [PMC9585344](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9585344/).

### Inferences
- `epubs.icar.org.in` is the single most valuable primary-source repository identified for this app's purposes: it is ICAR's own peer-reviewed journal platform (Indian Phytopathological Journal, Indian Journal of Nematology, Indian Journal of Agricultural Sciences, etc.) and yields crop-specific, dose-specific, citable biocontrol findings — a categorically better source than blog/commercial aggregators.
- The DPPQS "Major Uses of..." bio-insecticide document is the right *regulatory* complement to ICAR journal findings (it reflects what is **officially registered and labeled**, including dose/dilution/waiting period, versus what is **experimentally studied**), but its content must be independently re-verified since this session's summary of it came from a search snippet, not a direct read.
- Where this app currently relies on "generic web citations" (per the task's stated concern), the biggest quality upgrade available is to replace commercial dose claims (e.g., napanta.com-style listings) with epubs.icar.org.in journal citations and, once verified, the official DPPQS bio-insecticide PDF.

## What do international bodies like FAO document on biopesticides/organic crop protection relevant to South Asian agriculture?

### Takeaway
FAO has relevant institutional material at the regional (Asia-Pacific) level — through the **Asia-Pacific Plant Protection Commission (APPPC)**, periodic regional plant-protection outlooks, and historical biological-control guidance — but no single, easily identified FAO flagship publication specifically titled around "biopesticides for South Asia" was confirmed in this session. The clearest concrete FAO-linked artifact found is the **2022 "Plant protection outlook in the Asia-Pacific region"** report; a FAO-APPPC-organized regional workshop on biologicals also ran in 2025.

For the app's sourcing purposes, the 2022 FAO Asia-Pacific plant-protection outlook is the best-identified anchor citation, but its specific biopesticide/organic-treatment content needs direct verification before quoting doses or claims from it.

## Is there a reputable searchable database of biopesticide/biocontrol products registered in India (a biopesticide equivalent of CIBRC's chemical registry)?

### Takeaway
The registry function lives inside **CIBRC (Central Insecticides Board & Registration Committee)** itself, under the **Directorate of Plant Protection, Quarantine & Storage (DPPQS, ppqs.gov.in)** — there is no separate, independently-branded "biopesticide registry" website; instead, biopesticides are a *subset* of the same insecticide-registration regime (biopesticides are legally regulated as insecticides under India's Insecticides Act, 1968), surfaced through CIBRC's own site sections (e.g., "Bio-pesticide Registrant," "Registered Products") and periodic DPPQS guidance PDFs. Registered-biopesticide *counts* vary significantly by source and by what is being counted (species vs. formulations vs. "types"), so any single number should be treated cautiously and re-verified before quoting.

### Cited Findings
- The official regulator is **CIB&RC**, housed within **DPPQS (ppqs.gov.in)**; the site has a CIB&RC menu with sections including **"Bio-pesticide Registrant"** and **"Registered Products,"** plus a published **"GUIDELINES FOR REGISTRATION"** page — [ppqs.gov.in/divisions/cib-rc/guidelines](https://ppqs.gov.in/divisions/cib-rc/guidelines); [ppqs.gov.in/faq/faqcib-rc](https://ppqs.gov.in/faq/faqcib-rc). **Could not directly verify the live contents of the Registered Products / Bio-pesticide Registrant pages in this session** (WebFetch DNS failure on ppqs.gov.in) — the existence of these menu sections is confirmed via search-index snippets, but their current data was not read.
- A **"Ready Reckoner of CIBRC"** PDF is published on ppqs.gov.in, intended as a consolidated regulatory quick-reference — [ppqs.gov.in ready reckoner PDF](https://ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/ready_reckoner_of_cibrc_0.pdf) (existence confirmed via search index; content not read in this session).
- The periodically updated **"Major Uses of Pesticides/Insecticides/Bio-Insecticides"** PDF (editions dated 31.03.2024 and 30.09.2025 found) functions as the practical, citable "bio-insecticide list" with crop/pest/dose/dilution/waiting-period fields, per the earlier section — [ppqs.gov.in PDF](https://ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/major_uses_of_pesticides_insecticides_bio_insecticides_as_on_31.03.2024.pdf).
- **Registered-biopesticide counts conflict across sources**, underscoring that no single authoritative public count was confirmed: an NBAIR (ICAR-National Bureau of Agricultural Insect Resources) presentation hosted on chemicals.gov.in states only **"~14 biopesticides"** / **"12 types of biopesticides"** registered with CIBRC — [chemicals.gov.in / IPFT talk by Venkatesan, NBAIR](https://chemicals.gov.in/sites/default/files/Reports/IPFT_talk_-Venkatesan__NBAIR__Bangalore%5B1%5D.pdf); whereas a 2019 paper indexed on the ICAR Krishi repository (citing the Journal of Invertebrate Pathology) reports **"at least 15 microbial species and 970 microbial formulations"** registered through CIBRC — [krishi.icar.gov.in record](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/handle/123456789/46834). These numbers are **not directly comparable** (species counts vs. product/formulation counts vs. "types") and both should be treated as approximate/dated rather than as the current 2026 figure.
- **NIPHM Hyderabad** separately holds statutory **Central Insecticide Laboratory functions for biopesticide testing under Section 16 of the Insecticide Act, 1968** (repeated from the first section) — [niphm.gov.in](https://niphm.gov.in/Training/qcmb26.pdf) — meaning NIPHM is part of the official quality-control chain for registered biopesticides, alongside CIBRC's registration role.

### Inferences
