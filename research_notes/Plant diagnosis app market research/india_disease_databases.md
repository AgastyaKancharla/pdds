# India / Andhra Pradesh Crop Disease & Pest Data Sources

## What does ICAR publish that catalogs crop diseases/pests and management, and is it accessible online?

### Takeaway
ICAR's disease/pest knowledge is scattered across dozens of institute- and crop-specific technical bulletins, AICRP (All India Coordinated Research Project) pest-surveillance reports, and IPM extension folders — almost all distributed as individual PDFs, not as any unified structured database or API. The most consistent single access point is the **KRISHI repository** (krishi.icar.gov.in), ICAR's institutional document repository, but it is a document index with inconsistent metadata, not a queryable disease/pest dataset.

### Cited Findings
- KRISHI (krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/...) hosts individual technical bulletins such as "Weather based diseases and pests of rice and their management" (NRRI Bulletin No. 152), a diagnostic pocket guide to pests/diseases of ornamental crops (Directorate of Floricultural Research, 2019), Technical Bulletin No. 98 from Central Plantation Crops Research Institute on coconut pest/disease community extension, and Technical Bulletin No. 67 from Central Coastal Agricultural Research Institute on pheromone-based insect pest management — [krishi.icar.gov.in](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/handle/123456789/19694?mode=full), [krishi.icar.gov.in](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/handle/123456789/8708)
- ICAR-CICR (cotton) published a 126-page "Illustrative guide for integrated management for Cotton Pests, Diseases and Nematodes" (2026) as a direct PDF download on its own site, plus an 11-part "Crop Protection Folder" technical bulletin series on individual cotton pests (thrips, whiteflies, American bollworm, red/dusky cotton stainers, defoliators, natural enemies) archived in KRISHI — [CICR guide PDF](https://cicr.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/CICR_Illustrative-guide-for-integrated-management-for-Cotton-Pests-Diseases-and-Nematodes.pdf), [krishi.icar.gov.in](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/handle/123456789/14006)
- CICR also publishes standalone advisory PDFs directly off its live domain, e.g. a whitefly advisory at cicr.icar.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/PA_24_Whitefly.pdf, confirming at least two live CICR domains (cicr.org.in and cicr.icar.gov.in) both serve bulletin PDFs — [PA_24_Whitefly.pdf](https://cicr.icar.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/PA_24_Whitefly.pdf)
- ICAR-IIHR (horticulture, Bengaluru) does not appear to publish a crop-disease catalog page per se in these searches; its most visible recent public-facing crop-protection output is a 2023 "Hot Water Technology" postharvest treatment for mango combining anthracnose and fruit-fly control (55°C water for 20 min + 5% salt dip for 30 min), publicized via icar.org.in rather than a structured dataset — [icar.org.in](https://icar.org.in/en/icar-iihrs-hot-water-technology-boost-export-south-indian-mangoes)
- NBAIR (National Bureau of Agricultural Insect Resources) runs **CPOR** ("Crop Pest Observatory Report") — periodic PDF bulletins (e.g. "CPOR August 2020.pdf", "CPOR November 2020.pdf") hosted at databases.nbair.res.in/AICRP/, which log district-level pest incidence (e.g., fall armyworm infestation % by mandal in Vizianagaram district, AP) — these are the closest thing found to a recurring, semi-structured national pest-surveillance feed, but still PDF, not an API or database table — [CPOR Aug 2020](https://databases.nbair.res.in/AICRP/admin/upload/CPOR%20%20August%20%202020.pdf), [CPOR Nov 2020](https://databases.nbair.res.in/AICRP/admin/upload/CPOR%20November%202020.pdf)
- NBAIR also conducts and publishes ad hoc outbreak survey reports, e.g. a January 2023 chilli-thrips (Thrips parvispinus) survey covering Telangana and Andhra Pradesh fields, again as a standalone PDF — [nbair.res.in survey](https://www.nbair.res.in/sites/default/files/2023-02/Chilli%20thrips%20survey%20report%20-%20Jan%202023.pdf)
- ICAR-Indian Institute of Maize Research (IIMR, aicrpmaize.icar.gov.in / iimr.icar.gov.in) lists Turcicum leaf blight management guidance (Mancozeb spray schedule) on its site, again as institute web content/PDF rather than structured data — [aicrpmaize.icar.gov.in](https://aicrpmaize.icar.gov.in/?p=1163)
- ICAR-National Research Centre for Banana (NRCB, Tiruchirapalli) runs ongoing research projects on banana fungal/bacterial diseases and BBTV (bunchy top virus), with at least one 2016 KRISHI-archived publication "Management of leaf spot disease in banana" (Thangavelu & Mustaffa) — [NRCB project listing](https://www.indiascienceandtechnology.gov.in/research/investigation-fungal-and-bacterial-diseases-banana-and-their-management), [krishi.icar.gov.in](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/handle/123456789/76633)
- ICAR-Central Coastal Agricultural Research Institute (CCARI, Goa) publishes a turmeric "DSS" (decision support) web page on rhizome rot covering causal agent (Pythium spp.), symptoms, and Trichoderma harzianum seed/soil treatment — [ccari.icar.gov.in/dss/turmaric.html](https://ccari.icar.gov.in/dss/turmaric.html)

### Inferences
- There is no evidence of a single ICAR-wide structured/API-accessible disease-pest database; the realistic integration pattern is: scrape/ingest individual institute bulletin PDFs (KRISHI repository + individual institute domains like cicr.org.in, iimr.icar.gov.in, nrcb's domain, ccari.icar.gov.in) and extract structured fields manually or via document parsing.
- NBAIR's CPOR bulletins are the best candidate for a recurring, semi-regular, district-level pest-incidence feed relevant to Andhra Pradesh, but they require manual PDF parsing and are not guaranteed to continue being published on a fixed schedule (gaps in dates were found: Aug 2020, Nov 2020, no confirmed 2024-2026 issues located).
- Each ICAR institute (CICR for cotton, NRRI/CRRI for rice, IIMR for maize, NRCB for banana, CCARI/IISR-adjacent for turmeric, IIHR for horticulture broadly) is a separate website with its own publication conventions — building a catalog means treating each institute as its own ingestion source rather than one unified pipeline.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether ICAR-IIHR has a dedicated, browsable crop-disease catalog page (as opposed to news/press content) — searches surfaced only the hot-water-treatment press item, not a systematic IIHR disease database.
- Could not confirm the current (2025-2026) status/existence of AICRP annual reports as consolidated, downloadable PDFs with pest/disease data by crop and state — only fragments (CPOR bulletins, institute press pages) were found, not the AICRP reports themselves.
- Did not locate any ICAR API or open-data endpoint (REST/JSON) for pest or disease records; all located ICAR material is HTML pages or PDF files.

## What does ANGRAU publish specifically for AP crops (Vyavasaya Panchangam etc.), and how comprehensive/accessible is it?

### Takeaway
ANGRAU's flagship AP-specific farmer resource is the annual **Vyavasaya Panchangam** (agriculture almanac), a physical/sold book (priced ~Rs. 310) released yearly at Ugadi covering package-of-practices recommendations across the state's major crops in a farmers'-almanac format; it does not appear to be freely downloadable as an open PDF, and no structured digital/online version of its disease-pest content was located. ANGRAU also publishes KVK activity reports and E-Newsletters as PDFs on angrau.ac.in, which occasionally summarize advisory content but are not disease-catalog documents.

### Cited Findings
- The Vyavasaya Panchangam 2024-25 edition is described in ANGRAU's own April-June 2024 E-Newsletter as a "zodiac-format guide with recommendations on farming practices and technologies for the state's major crops" — [ANGRAU E-Newsletter Apr-Jun 2024](https://angrau.ac.in/downloads/Publications/E_News/E-Newsletter%20Vol.%208(2)%20April-June%202024.pdf)
- The 2025-26 edition was released at Ugadi by the Andhra Pradesh Chief Minister, per ANGRAU's Jan-Mar 2025 newsletter; the 2026-27 edition was likewise released, per the Jan-Mar 2026 newsletter — [ANGRAU Jan-Mar 2025](https://angrau.ac.in/downloads/Publications/E_News/E-Newsletter%20Jan-March-04-04-2025.pdf), [ANGRAU Jan-Mar 2026](https://angrau.ac.in/downloads/Publications/E_News/E-Newsletter%20Jan%20-%20March.26%20Final.pdf)
- ANGRAU sells the Panchangam as a physical book at Rs. 310/copy per its publications page — [angrau.ac.in publications](https://angrau.ac.in/downloads/Publications/E_News/E-Newsletter%20Jan-March-04-04-2025.pdf) (price cited index 1-5 of that newsletter)
- A near-identical almanac, "Vyavasaaya Diksoochi" / "Vyavasaaya Panchangam", is published by Telangana's PJTSAU covering "package-of-practices for all crops in the vernacular language," and is noted as popular in parts of Andhra Pradesh too, suggesting the two states' universities (both descended from the original undivided-AP agricultural university) run parallel, overlapping almanac programs — [pjtsau.edu.in publications](https://pjtsau.edu.in/publications.html), [pjtau.edu.in Diksoochi](https://pjtau.edu.in/pjtsau-vyavasaaya-diksoochi.html)
- ANGRAU runs individual Krishi Vigyan Kendras across AP districts (e.g., Dr. K.L. Rao KVK at Garikapadu, Krishna district, ICAR-funded); their activity reports are posted as PDFs on angrau.ac.in but function as program reports, not crop-disease catalogs — [ANGRAU KVK Garikapadu PDF](https://angrau.ac.in/downloads/KVK/KVK,%20Garikapadu%20activities.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Panchangam is the single most AP-comprehensive, multi-crop farmer advisory product identified in this research, but its primary distribution channel is a paid printed book rather than an open online database or even a freely downloadable PDF — meaning it cannot currently be scraped/ingested without physically acquiring/digitizing a copy.
- Because the Panchangam is described as covering "major crops" broadly (not a disease-specific document), its pest/disease content is likely only a subsection of a much larger package-of-practices publication, with the level of per-disease detail uncertain until an actual copy is obtained.

### Gaps
- Could not find a freely accessible online version (PDF or web page) of the Vyavasaya Panchangam's actual content — only secondary mentions of its existence and release ceremonies in ANGRAU newsletters.
- Could not confirm whether ANGRAU publishes any other dedicated, AP-crop-specific disease/pest advisory document online (e.g., a "plant protection" portal section); no such page surfaced in searches of angrau.ac.in.
- Could not determine whether any digitized/searchable version of the Panchangam or ANGRAU's broader extension advisories exists (e.g., via a mobile app or SMS service) beyond the physical book.

## What is CIBRC, and is its "Major Uses of Pesticides" registry a searchable database, static PDF, or something else? Real URL structure and programmatic access?

### Takeaway
CIBRC (Central Insecticides Board & Registration Committee) operates under the Directorate of Plant Protection, Quarantine & Storage (ppqs.gov.in), and its "Major Uses of Pesticides" list is **not a searchable database or API** — it is a set of static, separately-uploaded PDF documents (one per pesticide category: insecticides/bio-insecticides, fungicides, herbicides, plant growth regulators), each listing registered products with crop, target pest, dose/ha, dilution, and waiting period in tabular form inside the PDF. There is no confirmed query interface, URL parameter scheme, or JSON/CSV export; programmatic access would require PDF-table scraping of whichever document covers the needed pesticide category.

### Cited Findings
- The confirmed, currently-indexed PDF is "major_uses_of_pesticides_insecticides_bio_insecticides_as_on_31.03.2024.pdf" hosted at ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/ — a static file path, not a query endpoint — [ppqs.gov.in PDF](https://ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/major_uses_of_pesticides_insecticides_bio_insecticides_as_on_31.03.2024.pdf)
- The document is compiled "on the basis of available information for guidance and not for legal purposes," is organized by major uses of bio-insecticides and public-health uses, and each entry gives crop, target pest, dose per hectare, dilution in water, and waiting period — [same PDF, per search summary]
- A public notice on ppqs.gov.in confirms separate major-use lists exist for insecticides, fungicides, herbicides, plant growth regulators, and bio-pesticides (insecticides and fungicides) as distinct uploaded documents, and that stakeholders were invited to report errors within 15 days of each upload — [ppqs.gov.in public notice](https://ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/public_notice-updation.pdf)
- The ppqs.gov.in site has a navigable "Major Uses of Pesticides" page nested under a CIBRC menu, alongside a separate "Registered Products" page/section — [ppqs.gov.in CIB&RC guidelines page](https://ppqs.gov.in/divisions/cib-rc/guidelines?page=4)
- An older version of essentially the same list (dated 1 February 2023) is also mirrored/hosted on at least one state government site (Odisha), confirming these PDFs circulate as copied static files rather than being fetched live from a central API — [odishaagrilicense.nic.in mirror](https://odishaagrilicense.nic.in/public/actsRules/CIB%20Pesticides.pdf1.pdf)
- A secondary source (Krishi Jagran) cites the same ppqs.gov.in page and notes that, as of November 2020, there were 295 registered pesticide active ingredients in India — [krishijagran.com](https://krishijagran.com/crop-care/crop-protection-for-the-farmers/?amp=1)
- CIBRC also publishes a "Citizen Charter" PDF describing its registration process/guidelines — [citizen_charter_of_cibrc_0.pdf](https://ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/citizen_charter_of_cibrc_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- To programmatically use CIBRC data for a specific product/crop/pest combination, the realistic approach is: (1) download the relevant category PDF (insecticide/bio-insecticide, fungicide, herbicide, or PGR) from ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/, (2) run PDF table-extraction (e.g., via a tool like pdfplumber/camelot) to get rows of {pesticide, crop, target pest, dose, dilution, waiting period}, and (3) refresh periodically since ppqs.gov.in re-uploads dated snapshots (e.g., "as on 31.03.2024") rather than maintaining a live updating record.
- There is no evidence of a REST API, downloadable CSV/Excel, or search box for this specific registry — any claim of "searchable CIBRC database" should be treated as false; it is fundamentally a static-document publishing pattern.

### Gaps
- Did not directly fetch/open the PDF to confirm exact column structure or completeness (findings here are from search-result summaries of the PDF's described structure, not a direct read) — a direct WebFetch/PDF open of ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/major_uses_of_pesticides_insecticides_bio_insecticides_as_on_31.03.2024.pdf would be needed to verify exact schema before building an ingestion script.
- Could not confirm current URLs/dates for the fungicide, herbicide, and PGR major-use PDFs specifically (only the insecticide/bio-insecticide one was directly found); these likely exist at similarly-named paths under ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/ but were not individually verified.
- No information found on whether ppqs.gov.in offers any machine-readable "Registered Products" search (separate from Major Uses of Pesticides) — this was mentioned as a separate menu item but not explored.

## Other Indian government open-data sources (data.gov.in, KVK portals, state ag dept advisories, mKisan archive)

### Takeaway
No direct crop-disease dataset was found on data.gov.in itself in this search; the most promising confirmed Indian government open dataset is **AIKosh** (aikosh.indiaai.gov.in, India AI's open-data platform), which hosts a structured pest-surveillance dataset (state/district/sub-district/village/crop/pest/image-URL fields) and a farmer-query dataset. mKisan (mkisan.gov.in) is a real, large-scale SMS/IVR advisory system run by the Ministry of Agriculture, but it is a one-way broadcast tool for registered government officials to send messages to farmers — it is not confirmed to expose a public archive of past advisory messages. Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) crop-disease advisories exist only as scattered PDFs/news items (ANGRAU KVK pages, Krishi Jagran articles, Greenalerts.in republications of IMD agromet bulletins), with no single AP-wide KVK disease-advisory portal identified. Saagu Baagu (Telangana, chilli-focused AI pilot) is a real, WEF-documented digital advisory program using a Telugu WhatsApp chatbot (Digital Green + Glific), but its underlying disease/pest knowledge content is not published or publicly documented in structured form.

### Cited Findings
- AIKosh hosts an "npss_pest_surveillance" dataset described with fields State, District, SubDistrict, Village, Crop, Pest, and Image URL, and a separate "farmer_query_signal_intent_dataset" covering crop disease, pest control, weather, and market-price queries in Hindi — [aikosh.indiaai.gov.in pest surveillance](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/home/datasets/details/npss_pest_surveillance.html), [aikosh.indiaai.gov.in farmer query](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/home/datasets/details/farmer_query_signal_intent_dataset.html)
- No data.gov.in URL appeared in direct search results for "crop disease pest dataset agriculture"; most hits were non-Indian (South Korea's data.go.kr pest info dataset, Ethiopia's data.moa.gov.et, Australia's NSW DPI crop disease surveillance data, and Mendeley/IEEE image datasets for Ghana crop pests) — [search result summary, no data.gov.in link found]
- mKisan (mkisan.gov.in) was inaugurated 16 July 2013 by the President of India; as of a 2019 parliamentary reply, 2,462.37 crore SMSs had been sent to farmers since launch; it offers SMS, Pull-SMS, IVR, and extreme-weather alerts in 12 languages including Telugu, but sending access is restricted to registered government officials only — [mkisan.gov.in About](https://mkisan.gov.in/Home/About), parliamentary reply cited via [eparlib.nic.in](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/951371/1/AU1714.pdf)
- No dedicated public SMS-advisory archive page was found on mkisan.gov.in in this search — the site's visible structure (readme, pushsms pages) is oriented toward officials sending messages, not a browsable historical archive for the public — [mkisan.gov.in readme](https://mkisan.gov.in/alpha/readme.aspx)
- Greenalerts.in republishes "Agricultural Advisories for Andhra Pradesh and Telangana" sourced from the India Meteorological Department's National Agromet Advisory Service Bulletin, including zone-specific disease guidance (e.g., tomato powdery mildew fungicide doses) — this is a third-party aggregator, not an official government portal — [greenalerts.in](https://www.greenalerts.in/agricultural-advisories-for-andhra-pradesh-and-telangana/)
- Saagu Baagu's chilli pilot in Khammam district (Telangana), launched with WEF/AI4AI backing, reported (per WEF) a 21% increase in chilli yield per acre and a 9% reduction in pesticide use among ~7,000 participating farmers; delivery is via a Telugu-language WhatsApp chatbot (Digital Green, built on the Glific platform); the program has since expanded to 500,000 farmers across chickpeas, cotton, groundnuts, rice, and turmeric — [weforum.org](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2024/01/ai-for-agriculture-in-india/), [affairscloud.com](https://affairscloud.com/telangana-govt-wef-launched-saagu-baagu-project-phase-1-insights-report)
- KVKs operate in Andhra Pradesh under ANGRAU (e.g., Dr. K.L. Rao KVK, Garikapadu, Krishna district), but no centralized/searchable AP KVK disease-advisory portal was found; identified KVK-related material was limited to individual activity-report PDFs and Telangana-side (PJTSAU) KVK training-visit pages — [ANGRAU KVK PDF](https://angrau.ac.in/downloads/KVK/KVK,%20Garikapadu%20activities.pdf), [PJTAU KVK Kothagudem](https://www.pjtau.edu.in/kvk-kothagudem-training-pest-diseases-major-crops-08-02-2022.html)

### Inferences
- For building a crop-disease catalog, AIKosh's pest-surveillance dataset (with crop/pest/location/image fields) is the most structurally promising open Indian government-linked dataset found, worth directly inspecting for AP coverage and schema completeness before data.gov.in is ruled out entirely.
- A direct, targeted search of data.gov.in itself (its internal search, not general web search) is still worth doing before concluding it has nothing — general web search engines often fail to index data.gov.in's internal catalog pages well.
- Saagu Baagu demonstrates that India-based digital crop advisory at state scale is real and AP/Telangana-relevant, but its actual disease/pest knowledge base is proprietary to the Digital Green/Glific deployment and not published as open reference data.

### Gaps
- Did not perform a direct, in-portal search of data.gov.in (only general web search), so cannot rule out that AP- or crop-disease-specific datasets exist there under names/categories not surfaced by this search.
- Could not confirm whether mkisan.gov.in has any public archive or API for historical advisory message content (only confirmed its official-sender-facing design).
- Could not identify a single, current, centralized Andhra Pradesh state Department of Agriculture crop-disease advisory web portal (as distinct from KVK pages, ANGRAU newsletters, and third-party aggregators like Greenalerts.in) — this may not exist publicly, or may exist under a different name not found by this search.

## Most agriculturally significant diseases/pests per crop for Andhra Pradesh (beyond the current single condition each)

### Takeaway
For all ten crops, authoritative or AP-specific sources support at least 3 additional economically important diseases/pests beyond whatever single condition is currently cataloged; coverage quality (AP-specific vs. generic/other-state) varies by crop, and is noted per crop below.

### Cited Findings
**Rice** (AP-specific survey + general ICAR/NRRI sourcing)
- Sheath blight: an AP (Nellore/Chittoor districts) rabi 2020-21 survey measured severity of 6.11%–13.44%, with yield loss estimates elsewhere cited at 11%–58% — [sasapjas.org survey](https://sasapjas.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/09-Kalavathi.pdf)
- Blast — described generically as "one of the most destructive diseases of rice," found in both upland and lowland systems — [opentextbooks.colvee.org](https://opentextbooks.colvee.org/integrateddiseasemanagement/wp-content/uploads/sites/35/2023/12/Rice-diseases-and-their-management.pdf)
- Bacterial leaf blight — yield losses of 20%–50% cited (Philippines source, not AP-specific) — [pestrisk.da.gov.ph](https://pestrisk.da.gov.ph/?p=57)
- Brown planthopper — a 2024 Osmania University (Telangana-adjacent) study names blast, bacterial leaf blight, and brown planthopper as the "most potential threats" to rice productivity in the region — [microbiologyjournal.org](https://microbiologyjournal.org/download/90621)
- ICAR-NRRI's own bulletin (No. 152) explicitly covers "weather based diseases and pests of rice" as a named management resource — [krishi.icar.gov.in](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/handle/123456789/31542?mode=full) (implied by search listing)

**Chilli** (AP-specific, Krishna/Nandyal-area studies + NBAIR survey)
- Thrips (now largely the invasive *Thrips parvispinus*) — an NBAIR January 2023 survey confirmed outbreak-level infestation across Telangana and AP fields since October 2021, and a Krishna district (AP) study found thrips causing upward leaf curling ("murda disease") with IPM reducing damage from 16.0% (farmer practice) to 9.5% — [nbair.res.in](https://www.nbair.res.in/sites/default/files/2023-02/Chilli%20thrips%20survey%20report%20-%20Jan%202023.pdf), [phytojournal.com](https://www.phytojournal.com/archives/2019/vol8issue4/PartAY/8-4-584-112.pdf)
- Chilli leaf curl virus complex — managed in the same ICAR IPM module alongside thrips/anthracnose/mites/whitefly via imidacloprid dips, abamectin sprays, and rouging of infected plants — [krishi.icar.gov.in IPM module](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/handle/123456789/53350?mode=full)
- Anthracnose (fruit rot, *Colletotrichum* spp.) — covered in the same integrated module (carbendazim seed treatment, copper oxychloride spray) — [krishi.icar.gov.in](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/handle/123456789/53350?mode=full)
- Mites and whitefly — both named as co-targets of chilli IPM trials in IIVR research comparing Kashi varieties — [krishi.icar.gov.in](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/handle/123456789/50986)

**Cotton** (mixed AP-specific survey + general CICR sourcing)
- Whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*) — a 2023-24 ANGRAU survey in Nandyal/Kurnool districts (AP) recorded 6.66 whitefly/3 leaves at Dornipadu mandal in August, crossing the economic threshold level (ETL); CICR describes whitefly as feeding on 500+ plant species and vectoring multiple viral diseases, with yield losses of 15-30% — [sasapjas.org](https://sasapjas.org/?p=5691), [cicr.icar.gov.in whitefly advisory](https://cicr.icar.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/PA_24_Whitefly.pdf)
- Pink bollworm (*Pectinophora gossypiella*) — same AP survey covered pink bollworm alongside whitefly; pink bollworm is independently flagged nationally for Cry1Ac/Cry2Ab Bt-resistance development — [sasapjas.org](https://sasapjas.org/?p=5691), [icac.org CICR presentation](https://icac.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/05-AVSNagrare-CRDN-16-Sept-2017.pdf)
- Cotton leaf curl virus (CLCuV) — a CICR scientist states there are "no control measures" for the virus itself, so management is entirely via controlling the whitefly vector — [tribuneindia.com](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/community/leaf-curl-disease-detected-in-cotton-crop-278287)
- Leafhoppers/jassids and thrips — also recorded above ETL in the same Nandyal/Kurnool AP survey, alongside whitefly and pink bollworm — [sasapjas.org](https://sasapjas.org/?p=5691)

**Groundnut** (strong AP-specific sourcing, including ANGRAU/Bapatla and Nellore/Tirupati trial data)
- Tikka leaf spot (early + late leaf spot, *Cercospora arachidicola* / *Phaeoisariopsis personata*) and rust (*Puccinia arachidis*) — a 2024 Nellore district (AP) demo trial found rust at 16% in farmer practice vs 5% in demo (fungicide-treated) plots; combined foliar-disease attack (leaf spots + rust) can cause yield losses exceeding 70% per ICRISAT — [oar.icrisat.org bulletin](https://oar.icrisat.org/411/1/58373.pdf) (per search synthesis); Nellore 2024 trial cited in search summary
- Sowing-date sensitivity of tikka/late leaf spot was demonstrated in a Tirupati (AP) study — optimal rabi sowing window of 20 Nov-5 Dec minimized severity — [search summary of Tirupati study]
- Collar rot (*Aspergillus niger*), stem rot (*Sclerotium rolfsii*), and bud necrosis disease (groundnut bud necrosis virus) — all detected in a 1999 AP/Karnataka/Tamil Nadu multi-state field survey alongside the leaf spots and rust — [oar.icrisat.org, "Changing Scenario of Groundnut Diseases"](https://oar.icrisat.org/2236)
- An ANGRAU Bapatla (AP) screening of 42 genotypes identified resistant lines: 9 genotypes resistant to early leaf spot, 6 each to late leaf spot and rust — [arccjournals.com](https://arccjournals.com/journal/legume-research-an-international-journal/ARCC3957) (per search synthesis)

**Maize** (AP district-level surveillance data + ICAR-IIMR guidance)
- Fall armyworm (*Spodoptera frugiperda*) — NBAIR's CPOR national pest-outbreak reports recorded moderate-to-severe infestation (8-22% in Aug 2020, 5-20% in Nov 2020) in Vizianagaram district, AP; first sighted in AP in August 2018 per Down To Earth reporting — [CPOR Aug 2020](https://databases.nbair.res.in/AICRP/admin/upload/CPOR%20%20August%20%202020.pdf), [CPOR Nov 2020](https://databases.nbair.res.in/AICRP/admin/upload/CPOR%20November%202020.pdf), [downtoearth.org.in](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/agriculture/fall-armyworm-attack-low-maize-yield-reduces-cattle-feed-63700)
- Turcicum leaf blight (*Exserohilum turcicum*) — ICAR-IIMR's own site recommends Mancozeb (2.5 g/l) or Dithane Z-78 (3 g/l) sprays at 7-10 day intervals from the knee-high stage — [aicrpmaize.icar.gov.in](https://aicrpmaize.icar.gov.in/?p=1163)

**Sugarcane** (weakest AP-specific sourcing found; mostly national-trend data)
- Fall armyworm — also reported attacking sugarcane; first found on sugarcane in Karnataka (2018), subsequently spreading to AP, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Bihar, Odisha per an ICAR Indian Farming article — [epubs.icar.org.in](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndFarm/article/download/116635/45290/304621)
- Early shoot borer — NBAIR's Aug 2020 CPOR recorded low infestation (<5%) in Visakhapatnam district, AP — [CPOR Aug 2020](https://databases.nbair.res.in/AICRP/admin/upload/CPOR%20%20August%20%202020.pdf)
- Red rot — national trend material notes it "continuing to affect crops in 2019-20 and 2020-21," but no AP-specific severity data was found — [global-agriculture.com crop protection trends](https://www.global-agriculture.com/crop-protection/major-pest-and-disease-trends-in-indian-agriculture-2015-16-to-2023-24/)
- Smut — no AP-specific data found in this search at all (gap, see below)

**Tomato** (general/Vikaspedia sourcing naming AP among affected states, not AP-quantified)
- Tomato leaf curl virus (ToLCV) — Vikaspedia lists AP among states where it occurs; vectored by *Bemisia tabaci* whitefly, with one adult able to spread it to ~20% of plants per an ICAR-linked study — [vikaspedia.in tomato pests](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/agriculture/crop-production/integrated-pest-managment/ipm-for-vegetables/ipm-strategies-for-tomato/tomato-pests?lgn=en)
- Early blight (*Alternaria solani*) — Vikaspedia lists AP among affected states; one Indian Horticulture review ranks it second only to leaf curl virus in importance, with yield loss estimates up to 50-80% cited in some reviews — [epubs.icar.org.in](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndHort/article/view/84679)
- Late blight (*Phytophthora infestans*) — also listed for AP by Vikaspedia, but no severity/incidence data for the state was found — [vikaspedia.in](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/agriculture/crop-production/integrated-pest-managment/ipm-for-vegetables/ipm-strategies-for-tomato/tomato-pests?lgn=en)

**Mango** (general ICAR/ICAR-IIHR sourcing, not AP-quantified)
- Anthracnose (*Colletotrichum gloeosporioides*) — ICAR-IIHR's 2023 hot-water postharvest protocol (55°C/20 min + 5% salt dip/30 min) manages anthracnose jointly with fruit fly; extension guidance also recommends carbendazim (0.1%) sprays twice at 15-day intervals during flowering, plus copper fungicide (0.3%) for foliar infection — [icar.org.in](https://icar.org.in/en/icar-iihrs-hot-water-technology-boost-export-south-indian-mangoes)
- Powdery mildew — named generically among major mango diseases in review literature, but no AP-specific or ICAR-IIHR-specific detail was retrieved in this search (gap)
- Mango malformation — a 2019 Indian Journal of Agricultural Sciences screening of 72 cultivars/107 hybrids found only 3 cultivars (Bhadauran, Ilaichi, Arka Nilanchal) fully malformation-resistant; prochloraz/benomyl sprays plus physical removal of affected panicles reduced incidence — [epubs.icar.org.in](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/download/87093/44065/294543)
- Fruit fly — jointly targeted by the same ICAR-IIHR hot-water protocol as anthracnose — [icar.org.in](https://icar.org.in/en/icar-iihrs-hot-water-technology-boost-export-south-indian-mangoes)

**Banana** (weak AP-specific sourcing; mostly ICAR-NRCB general research program info)
- Sigatoka leaf spot — ICAR-NRCB (Thayanur, Tamil Nadu) has an active fungal/bacterial-disease-management research project and a 2016 KRISHI-archived publication specifically on "Management of leaf spot disease in banana" (Thangavelu & Mustaffa), but no AP-specific incidence data was retrieved — [NRCB project](https://www.indiascienceandtechnology.gov.in/research/investigation-fungal-and-bacterial-diseases-banana-and-their-management), [krishi.icar.gov.in](https://krishi.icar.gov.in/jspui/handle/123456789/76633)
- Banana bunchy top virus (BBTV) — covered by a separate NRCB research project on banana viruses (host-BBTV interaction work), but no management specifics or AP data surfaced — [search summary]
- Panama wilt (*Fusarium oxysporum* f. sp. *cubense*) — only an indirect mention found: a news item stating NRCB would release two new banana varieties resistant to "wilt and viral diseases," without naming Panama wilt explicitly or giving AP incidence data — [itfnet.org](https://itfnet.org/v1/?p=20839)

**Turmeric** (moderate AP-specific sourcing via one survey + ICAR-CCARI/Vikaspedia)
- Leaf spot (*Colletotrichum capsici*) — causes brown spots on young leaves merging into patches; Vikaspedia/extension sources recommend zineb (0.3%) or Bordeaux mixture (1%) or mancozeb (0.2%) sprays — [en.vikaspedia.in turmeric](https://en.vikaspedia.in/agriculture/crop-production/package-of-practices/spices/turmeric)
- Rhizome rot (*Pythium* spp.) — an ICAR-associated survey found the highest disease incidence specifically in **Guntur district, Andhra Pradesh (32.22%)**; ICAR-CCARI recommends Trichoderma harzianum seed and soil treatment alongside mancozeb (0.3%) seed-rhizome treatment — [ccari.icar.gov.in/dss/turmaric.html](https://ccari.icar.gov.in/dss/turmaric.html)
- Shoot borer (*Conogethes punctiferalis*) — described as "the most serious pest of turmeric," boring into pseudostems; no specific insecticide dosage recommendation was retrieved in this search (gap) — [agridots.com turmeric production tech](https://agridots.com/courses/bsc-agriculture/horticulture/hort282-production-tech-of-spices-aromatic-medicinal-plantation-crops/02-turmeric)

### Inferences
- Groundnut, chilli, cotton, and turmeric have the best AP-district-level quantitative data among the ten crops (Nellore/Tirupati/Bapatla for groundnut; Krishna/Nandyal/Kurnool for chilli and cotton; Guntur for turmeric rhizome rot), likely reflecting ANGRAU's own research output being more discoverable for its core regional crops.
- Sugarcane, mango, banana, and tomato have the weakest AP-specific (as opposed to generic or other-state) sourcing in this search round — for a knowledge catalog, these four crops' entries would currently rely more on India-wide ICAR/extension guidance than confirmed AP incidence data.
- Fall armyworm is notable as a genuinely cross-cutting, AP-confirmed pest affecting both maize and sugarcane per NBAIR's own district-level CPOR surveillance — a useful "shared pest" case for a catalog's data model.

### Gaps
- No AP-specific incidence/severity data found for: sugarcane smut, mango powdery mildew, banana Panama wilt, banana BBTV, tomato late blight, or turmeric shoot borer dosage recommendations — these would need additional, more targeted searching (e.g. directly on ANGRAU's own research-journal publications or ICAR-IISR for turmeric) before being stated as AP-verified facts.
- Most severity/incidence percentages found date from single-year or multi-year-old trials (1999-2024 range) rather than a current, standardized annual state-wide AP crop health survey; no single authoritative "top N diseases per crop for AP, updated annually" list was located for any of the ten crops.
